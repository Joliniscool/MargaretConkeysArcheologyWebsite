Research

Gender & Feminist Archaeology

Meg's 1984 article with Janet Spector, “Archaeology and the Study of Gender,” is widely credited with opening the archaeology of gender as a field. With Joan Gero she edited Engendering Archaeology: Women and Prehistory (1991), and she has continued to write about how feminist practice changes the way archaeology is done.

Relevant publications

  • 1984Conkey, M. W., and J. D. Spector. Archaeology and the Study of Gender. Advances in Archaeological Method and Theory 7.
  • 1991Gero, J. M., and M. W. Conkey, eds. Engendering Archaeology: Women and Prehistory. Blackwell.
  • 1997Conkey, M. W., and J. M. Gero. Programme to Practice: Gender and Feminism in Archaeology. Annual Review of Anthropology 26.

Research

Interpreting “Paleolithic Art”

From her early work on the cave of Altamira as an aggregation site, Meg has asked what Upper Paleolithic imagery did for the people who made it, and has questioned whether “art” is the right word for it at all.

Relevant publications

  • 1980Conkey, M. W. The Identification of Prehistoric Hunter-Gatherer Aggregation Sites: The Case of Altamira. Current Anthropology 21(5).
  • 1987Conkey, M. W. New Approaches in the Search for Meaning? A Review of Research in “Paleolithic Art.” Journal of Field Archaeology 14(4).
  • 1997Conkey, M. W., O. Soffer, D. Stratmann, and N. G. Jablonski, eds. Beyond Art: Pleistocene Image and Symbol. California Academy of Sciences.
